In February 2012, Trayvon Martin, a 17-year-old, was shot and killed in Sanford, Florida, by George Zimmerman, a neighbourhood watch volunteer. In July 2013, a jury acquitted Zimmerman.

That night, the Oakland organiser Alicia Garza wrote a Facebook post about her reaction, ending with words of love to Black people and the line "our lives matter." Her friend Patrisse Cullors turned the idea into a hashtag: #BlackLivesMatter. Together with Opal Tometi, a Nigerian-American organiser, they began building it into a network.

## From hashtag to movement

In August 2014, Michael Brown, an 18-year-old, was shot and killed by a police officer in Ferguson, Missouri. Weeks of protest followed, and the slogan went with them, onto placards, into news coverage, and into the national conversation in the United States.

Over the next few years, the names kept coming. Then, on 25 May 2020, George Floyd was killed in Minneapolis by a police officer who knelt on his neck for more than nine minutes while a bystander filmed. The video spread around the world, and so did the protests. In the United States, they are counted among the largest in the country's history, and demonstrations took place on every continent except Antarctica. In 2021, the officer, Derek Chauvin, was convicted of murder.

## What the words mean

The slogan has been misread from the start. Supporters say "Black lives matter" was never a claim that other lives don't. It is a response to evidence that Black lives have been treated as if they matter less, in policing, in courts and in history. The "too" is implied.

Critics answered with "All lives matter." Supporters reply that this is true but misses the point, like answering someone who says their house is on fire by pointing out that every house matters.

## The arguments around it

It's worth separating three things that often get blurred: a slogan anyone can say, a decentralised movement of local groups and protesters, and the Black Lives Matter Global Network Foundation, a formal organisation.

Each has drawn criticism. The call to "defund the police," taken up by some activists, split even sympathetic observers: some meant moving money into social services, others meant abolition, and many voters opposed both. Some 2020 protests also involved riots and property damage, which critics pointed to and many organisers condemned.

The Foundation faced serious questions about its finances after raising around $90 million in 2020, including reporting on a $6 million property purchase. Cullors stepped down as executive director in 2021 while denying wrongdoing.

Supporters respond that a movement should not be judged only by one organisation's accounting, and that the core issue, how Black people are policed and treated, remains whether or not any one group handles money well.

## Why it reached us

Black Lives Matter is an American story, but it didn't stay in America. It was heard in London, Lagos, Rio and here in Accra, because the question underneath it is old and global: whose life is treated as valuable, by whom, and with what consequences.

Ghana's story runs from the forts on our coast to the diaspora that came from them, and the people in that video, on those streets, are part of it. Whatever you make of the organisations, the question deserves a serious answer.
